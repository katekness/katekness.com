Fascia, explained

Meet your fascia.

The connective tissue wrapped around every muscle, bone and organ you have. Quietly running the show.

Plain answers to the questions people ask most, from the author of Girl, Listen: Your Fascia Needs to Talk to You. Curious, not clinical, and not medical advice.

What is fascia?

Fascia is the connective tissue that wraps around and runs through everything in your body: every muscle, bone, nerve, blood vessel and organ. It’s mostly collagen, with a slippery fluid called hyaluronan between its layers so things can glide. Think of the thin, shiny film on a chicken breast, then imagine it everywhere, all connected.

What does fascia do?

It holds you together and lets you move. Fascia gives muscles something to pull against, lets layers slide past each other, carries nerves and blood vessels, and helps shape how you stand. It’s also full of nerve endings, so it’s part of how your body senses itself.

Is fascia the same as muscle?

No, but they work as a team. Muscle contracts to move you. Fascia surrounds and connects muscle (and everything else), passing force along and letting layers slide. That’s why what feels like a tight muscle often involves both.

Why does fascia get tight?

The usual suspects are not moving much, injury and inflammation. When tissue doesn’t move, its layers can stiffen and stop gliding as easily. That’s why the shoulder you never stretch starts to feel like cardboard. Long days at a desk count.

Can stress make fascia tight?

Stress shows up in the body, and fascia is wired into the conversation. Researchers have found nerve fibers from the stress side of your nervous system running through the fascia just under your skin. Exactly how stress changes tissue tension is still being studied, but the clenched jaw and lifted shoulders in a hard week aren’t your imagination.

Does fascia hold emotions?

You’ll see claims that fascia “stores” trauma or memories. That isn’t established science. What is true: fascia is richly wired with nerves, it responds to how you move and brace, and many people notice their bodies tighten in emotional moments. Listening to that is the whole point of the book, no magic required.

Does fascia need water?

Healthy fascia is less about chugging water and more about moving. The fluid between its layers stays slippery when you move and gets thicker when you don’t. So yes, drink your water, then get up and walk around. Both help.

How do you release tight fascia?

Gentle, regular movement is the best starting point: walking, stretching, yoga and changing positions often. Heat, massage and foam rolling help a lot of people feel looser. If something is painful, numb or won’t let up, that’s a job for a doctor or physical therapist, not the internet.

Why does my jaw clench?

The jaw is one of the most common places tension shows up, often while you’re concentrating or stressed and usually without noticing. Quick check: are your teeth touching right now? At rest, they’re generally meant to be slightly apart.
